# CLIENT INTELLIGENCE REPORT — Noor

---

## VERIFIED FACTS

1. **Business:** 42-seat fine dining restaurant, DIFC (Dubai International Financial Centre), Dubai, UAE
2. **Name meaning:** "Noor" = "light" in Arabic
3. **Chef:** Khalid Al-Rashid — trained at Noma (Copenhagen) and Gaggan (Bangkok), returned to Dubai 2023
4. **Cuisine:** Modern Emirati — ancestral ingredients (camel milk, dates, saffron, dried lime) + Nordic technique
5. **Format:** Single 12-course tasting experience, no à la carte, no menu choice — changes with lunar calendar (each new moon = new sequence)
6. **Price:** AED 1,200 per person (~$327 USD)
7. **Space:** Former jewelry vault in Gate Village, DIFC. Raw concrete walls, terrazzo floors, 14-foot ceilings, polished black granite table slabs (no tablecloths), one candle per table, partially visible kitchen through horizontal window
8. **Light installation:** Ceiling piece by Emirati artist Shaikha Al Mazrou — shifts color temperature through meal (warm amber → cool blue-white → amber)
9. **Operating history:** 8 months open. 3-month waitlist
10. **Press:** The National, TimeOut Dubai, Condé Nast Traveller Middle East
11. **Recognition:** One Michelin star (awarded June 2024)
12. **Policy:** No photography allowed during service
13. **Existing assets:** 12 professional photographs by Natelee Cocks (atmosphere + plating), custom weight-split wordmark ("NOOR" — thin N-O, heavy O-R), no video, no brand guidelines
14. **Budget:** Unlimited ("I will spend whatever is necessary")

---

## CLIENT CLAIMS

1. "I don't want a restaurant website." — Khalid explicitly rejects the category format
2. "Every restaurant website looks the same — hero photo, menu, about the chef, book a table." — Identifies the conventional template as the enemy
3. "I want someone to visit the site and feel what it is like to sit in my dining room at 8:47pm when the third course arrives and the light overhead shifts from gold to rose." — The goal is temporal, sensory transfer — not information delivery
4. "If the website cannot do that, I would rather have no website." — Failure condition is explicit: if the site feels like a restaurant site, it has failed
5. "I see Zuma's site, I see La Petite Maison's site, I see Nobu's site — they are all the same site with different logos." — Competitors are dismissed as interchangeable templates
6. "I spent two years finding the right stone for the tables. I will spend whatever is necessary for the right website." — The same obsessive material specificity applied to food and space will be applied to the website

---

## INFERRED OBSERVATIONS

1. **The no-photography policy is the brand's central stance.** Not photographing the food is not a restriction — it is a philosophical position: the experience cannot be captured, only lived. This implies the website should not try to "show" the experience either. (Evidence: policy is explicit, not apologetic; Khalid frames the goal as "feel" not "see")

2. **Light is the primary design material, not food.** The ceiling installation is the room's only decoration. Light shifts through the meal. The restaurant is named "light." The concept brief centers on light at a specific moment (8:47pm, gold to rose). Food is the craft; light is the identity. (Evidence: name, installation description, Khalid's specific sensory reference)

3. **The lunar calendar format implies cyclicality, not linearity.** The menu is not a list — it is a cycle. Each new moon resets. This temporal structure is unusual and central to the brand's philosophy. (Evidence: "changes with the lunar calendar")

4. **"Surrender" is the operative word.** Guests do not choose. The tasting format removes decision-making. The no-photography rule removes documentation. The experience demands presence. (Evidence: "Guests do not choose — they surrender to the kitchen's judgment")

5. **Khalid's training trajectory reveals a specific thesis.** Noma (ascetic Nordic, fermentation, foraging) + Gaggan (progressive Indian, molecular, theatrical) → Noor (ancestral Emirati ingredients, Nordic technique). The synthesis is: ancient ingredients treated with modern restraint. Not fusion — translation. (Evidence: named training restaurants, described cuisine)

6. **The space is anti-decorative by design.** Raw concrete, no tablecloths, no art except light, one candle. This is deliberate material austerity — the space does not compete with the food or the light. (Evidence: space description — every absence is a choice)

7. **The audience does not need education.** AED 5,000+/month dining budget, has eaten at Noma/EMP/Mirazur. These visitors know fine dining intimately. They do not need to be told what a tasting menu is, what Michelin means, or why the price is justified. The site can assume sophistication. (Evidence: explicit audience description)

8. **The 3-month waitlist means the site does not need to sell.** The restaurant is already oversubscribed. The website's job is not conversion in the conventional sense — it is to be worthy of the experience it represents. (Evidence: 3-month waitlist, unlimited budget)

---

## UNKNOWNS

1. **Does Khalid want a reservation system on the site, or does booking happen through a separate channel?** The waitlist suggests a concierge model, not an OpenTable widget. This affects whether the site has ANY transactional function.

2. **Can the 12 Natelee Cocks photographs be used on the site?** The no-photography policy during service may extend to the restaurant's public image. Khalid may prefer generated atmospheric imagery over documentary photography. This is a critical asset decision.

3. **Is the light installation documented in any form?** Video, time-lapse, or color data from the installation would be the most powerful asset possible — light IS the brand.

4. **What is the lunar calendar's relationship to Emirati culture specifically?** This may carry cultural significance beyond the culinary format — understanding this informs whether the lunar cycle is a structural metaphor or merely a scheduling mechanism.

5. **Does Khalid want the site in Arabic, English, or both?** DIFC is international but the brand is rooted in Emirati identity.

---

**NOTE:** The brief is unusually rich. The Client Claims are specific, opinionated, and philosophically grounded. The Unknowns are manageable. This report supports strong concept generation in Phase 2. The primary vulnerability is the no-photography tension: the brand's most powerful asset (the light experience) is the one thing that cannot be conventionally documented.
